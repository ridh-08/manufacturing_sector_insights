Transport Equipment: Scaling, Steady, and Structurally Sound

Primary ResearchNidhi Patel
Compiled byRiddhi Bhargava
SeriesSector Snapshots
Abstract

India's transport equipment sector operates 8,869 registered factories, employs over 17.9 lakh persons, and produced a Gross Value of Output of ₹16.27 lakh crore in 2023-24, more than two and a half times its 2014-15 level. Unlike several sectors in this series, transport equipment is not a story of scale without structural progress: on the Index of Industrial Production it posted the fastest latest-year growth (11.8%) of any sector under study and now sits at an all-time index high, its balance sheet has deleveraged sharply over the decade, and its investment pipeline has grown to over ₹8.45 lakh crore outstanding with one of the lowest stalled-project rates of any sector tracked. This paper sets out the sector's scale, output trajectory, trade position, cost and capital structure, firm-level dynamics, policy support and investment pipeline, principal production clusters, and state-level footprint, drawing on the Annual Survey of Industries, the Index of Industrial Production, CMIE, DGCI&S trade data, and the institute's internal cluster registry.

§1

The Scale

Transport equipment is one of the largest sectors in this series by output: 8,869 factories were registered under the Annual Survey of Industries in 2023-24, producing a combined Gross Value of Output of ₹16.27 lakh crore, up from ₹6.17 lakh crore in 2014-15, a 163.6% increase over the decade.[1]

§2

The Surge

Transport equipment is the standout performer on the Index of Industrial Production among the sectors in this series. Its 2025-26 index level of 157.76 is an all-time high, comfortably above its previous 2018-19 peak of 128.86, which it first cleared in 2023-24 (132.33) and has extended every year since. Latest-year growth (11.8%) is the fastest of any of the 12 sectors tracked, and the sector's average rank across index level, latest growth, and full-period CAGR (2.67) is the best of any sector in the composite ranking.[2](a)

This strength is a post-COVID story, not a decade-long one. Pre-COVID CAGR (2012-13 to 2019-20) was a modest 1.38%, and the sector took its hardest hit of any major downturn during COVID itself: the index fell 18.7% from 2019-20 to 2020-21, a sharper shock than textiles or the manufacturing average. What followed was an unusually strong recovery: post-COVID CAGR (2020-21 to 2025-26) runs at 12.04%, the second-highest of the 12 sectors, behind only Construction Material. On the "above/below Overall Manufacturing" classification, the sector was below the composite manufacturing index in all 14 years, a distinct measure from its rank among the 12 sectors: it means transport equipment's own trajectory has tracked persistently just under the broader manufacturing-wide benchmark set largely by other sectors' 2011-12 base, even while comfortably outranking most of them on growth.[2](a)

The Balance

Figures in this section are directional rather than exact. The underlying HS-to-NIC concordance maps a defined share of export and import value to each sector; the remainder is unclassified and excluded rather than estimated, so totals should be read as a close approximation of trade flows, not an exact reconciliation.(b)

Transport equipment runs a trade surplus, though a narrower one relative to output than textiles: ₹65,139 crore in FY24-25, on exports of ₹3,46,034 crore against imports of ₹2,80,895 crore. Import dependency is moderate at 44.8%. Export growth is the standout figure here: exports grew 24.7% year-on-year in FY24-25 against 12.0% import growth, and the five-year export CAGR (12.3%) outpaces the five-year import CAGR (7.4%). Leading export partners are the USA, UAE, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UK; leading import sources are the USA, UAE, China, Saudi Arabia and Switzerland.[3]

The product mix is more capital-goods-heavy than a pure automotive read would suggest: the single largest export line is aircraft over 15,000 kg unladen weight (₹46,823 crore), ahead of mid-size passenger vehicles and turbo-jet engines. Aircraft is also the single largest import line, at ₹1,04,810 crore, more than double the export figure for the same product line, indicating the sector's aviation trade is import-heavy even as its automotive trade runs a surplus.[3]

§4

The Structure

On an aggregate, all-companies basis, transport equipment carries a Raw Material Cost of 60.95% of sales and an Employee Cost of 6.05% of sales in 2024-25, materials-heavy but far less labour-intensive than textiles, consistent with an assembly-and-components industry rather than a workforce-heavy one. Capital intensity (Gross Fixed Assets ÷ Sales) stands at 0.45×, down from 0.52× a decade earlier, and well below textiles' 0.69×, suggesting comparatively efficient asset utilisation for a sector this large. Sales per employee has been broadly flat, ₹18.85 million in 2014-15 versus ₹21.13 million in 2024-25, with a dip to ₹13.98 million in the COVID year. R&D spend has fallen as a share of sales, from 0.79% in 2014-15 to 0.40% in 2024-25, even as absolute R&D spend has not kept pace with the sector's sales growth. Cross-sector comparison on these measures is deferred until aggregate-basis figures are available for all sectors in this series.[8]

§4.1 Returns, Leverage and Liquidity

The decade's financial trajectory is one of steady deleveraging alongside improving returns, a stronger and steadier version of the turnaround seen in textiles. PAT margin rose from 2.63% in 2014-15 to 7.21% in 2024-25 (with a COVID-year dip to 1.70%), ROE from 7.32% to 17.53%, and ROCE from 12.37% to 19.95%. Debt-to-Equity fell from 0.62× to 0.24×, and Interest Cover improved from 3.32× to 12.4×, among the strongest interest-coverage positions of any sector in this series. Current Ratio improved modestly, from 1.08 to 1.24. Unlike textiles, this sector's Free Cash Flow figures were not separately re-derived this session and are not reported here pending confirmation of the underlying Purchase-of-Fixed-Assets and Operating Cash Flow lines.[8](c)

§5

The Firms

Maruti Suzuki India has held the number one sales rank in this 20-firm panel continuously from FY14 through FY25, growing from ₹47,823 crore to ₹1,45,115 crore, a 203% increase, the sector's clearest decade-long constant at the top. Toyota Kirloskar Motor is the standout riser, climbing from 8th (₹15,873 crore) to 5th (₹64,332 crore), a 305% increase, the largest percentage gain of any firm in the panel. Chevrolet Sales India is the sector's clearest collapse: 18th in FY14 at ₹4,549 crore, down to 19th by FY25 at just ₹48 crore, a 99% decline that tracks General Motors' 2017 exit from the Indian passenger-vehicle market. Only one of the original 20 firms, Skoda Auto India Pvt Ltd, dropped out of continuous reporting, having merged into Skoda Auto Volkswagen India, a much lower churn rate than seen in textiles.[4]

Concentration has eased over the decade rather than risen, a different pattern from textiles. The top-3 firms' combined share was 44.3% in 2024-25, down from 53.8% in 2015-16; the sector's Herfindahl-Hirschman Index fell from 1,297 in 2015-16 to 989 by 2024-25, remaining in the "Competitive" band throughout and drifting toward more competition rather than less, likely reflecting new-entrant and EV-segment fragmentation rather than incumbent consolidation.[4],[6](d)

§6

The Support

The Production-Linked Incentive scheme's actual disbursement to transport equipment has scaled up sharply and is, in absolute terms, far larger than the textiles equivalent. CMIE's tracked PLI expenditure was ₹3,557 crore (Budget Estimate) and ₹404 crore (Revised Estimate) in 2024-25, rising to a Budget Estimate of ₹2,819 crore and Revised Estimate of ₹2,091 crore for 2025-26. This session's files do not include the Ministry Annual Report total multi-year scheme outlay figure used for textiles' PLI framing (₹10,683 crore there); that total is not stated here and should not be assumed, pending the relevant Ministry of Heavy Industries or NITI Aayog document.[8](e)

The sector is a persistent net importer of foreign exchange on a company-financials basis (distinct from the customs-based trade surplus in §3): Net Forex was −₹4,450 crore in 2024-25, negative throughout the decade except for a brief positive stretch in 2021-22 (+₹1,482 crore). Contribution to Exchequer (total taxes) stood at ₹51,459 crore in 2024-25, roughly 65% higher than the 2014-15 figure of ₹75,088 crore once adjusted for the sharp COVID-year fall to ₹18,053 crore in 2020-21.[8]

Observation

Actual PLI disbursement to transport equipment in 2024-25 (₹404 crore Revised Estimate) is roughly nine times the equivalent textiles figure (₹45 crore), consistent with the scheme's design emphasis on advanced automotive technology and components. Even so, disbursement remains a small fraction of the sector's ₹18.89 lakh crore 2024-25 sales.

§6.1 Investment Pipeline

Transport equipment carries by far the largest investment pipeline of any sector examined in this series to date. Total investment outstanding reached ₹8,45,019 crore in 2025-26 (250 projects), up from ₹3,64,634 crore in 2014-15, a 132% increase, and roughly six times the outstanding pipeline size of textiles. Growth has been broadly steady rather than a single-year step-change: the pipeline has grown in 9 of the last 11 years.[8]

The Implementation Stalled share of total outstanding investment (Table 2) has stayed consistently low across the full 12-year series, never exceeding 2.3%, and has fallen further to 0.69% by 2025-26. On ownership, new investment in 2025-26 was, as in textiles, overwhelmingly domestic and private: Indian Private Sector accounted for ₹85,985 crore of the ₹95,240 crore national total (90.3%), Foreign Private Sector for ₹6,755 crore (7.1%), and Central and State Government combined for just ₹2,500 crore (2.6%). State-level, the investment pipeline is tracked for the three leading GVO states, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, and Haryana, and it diverges sharply from the state-level GVO ranking in §7. Maharashtra carries by far the largest outstanding pipeline of the three, ₹1,80,515 crore in 2025-26, more than double Tamil Nadu's ₹85,334 crore, even though the two states are within 7% of each other on current GVO. Where It Is Made: Clusters

The institute's cluster registry logs four transport equipment clusters, ranked and matched almost exactly to the state-level GVO ranking in §7: Chennai (Tamil Nadu), Pune (Maharashtra), Gurugram-Manesar-Bawal (Haryana), and Sanand (Gujarat). All four have at least one anchor firm logged and a linkage verdict, the fullest cluster coverage of any sector examined in this series so far.[7]

§9

Summary

Transport equipment is the strongest all-round performer among the sectors examined in this series so far. Output more than doubled over the decade, the sector currently posts the fastest IIP growth of any sector tracked, leverage has fallen sharply while returns have risen, market concentration has eased rather than tightened, and the investment pipeline, the largest of any sector in this series, converts announcements into active projects at a far higher rate than textiles, with a stalled-project share under 1% in the latest year. The two clearest points of caution are a declining R&D-to-sales ratio despite rising output, and a persistent net-forex deficit on a company-financials basis even as the sector runs a customs-based trade surplus.

Methodological Notes

  • (a)This paper's IIP figures were checked against two source files. The first, an 11-sector "corrected" workbook consistent with the rest of this series, contained a data-integrity issue: four of its eight analysis tabs listed "Transport and Equipment" twice, with the second, spurious row's values matching the Overall Manufacturing (official) row instead. A second, 12-sector file (Chemicals and Pharmaceuticals reported separately rather than as a weighted composite) supplied for this paper did not have this duplication, and its Transport Equipment figures match the correct (non-duplicated) row from the first file exactly. This paper uses the second file's figures on that basis; because Transport Equipment is unaffected by the Chemicals/Pharmaceuticals split, its own numbers are unaffected either way, though its "of 11" vs "of 12" sector-count and rank-among-peers framing differs slightly from Textiles' framing (rank 3 of 11 there vs rank 4 of 12 here, on the level measure).
  • (b)Trade figures in §3 use the same HS-to-NIC concordance and directional-estimate caveat described in the Textiles paper; the specific coverage percentages for Transport Equipment were not independently re-confirmed in this session and should be checked against the source methodology tab before publication.
  • (c)Free Cash Flow (Net Operating Cash Flow minus Purchase of Fixed Assets) was reported for Textiles in §4.1 of that paper but is omitted here pending confirmation that the same two source lines are clean and complete for Transport Equipment in the underlying CMIE export.
  • (d)The 2014-15 HHI value (10,000) reflects an anomalous single-firm-reporting base year and is excluded from the trend discussion and Figure 8, consistent with the same exclusion applied to Textiles' 2014-15 figure in that paper.
  • (e)Unlike the Textiles paper, this paper does not state a total multi-year PLI scheme outlay figure for Transport Equipment (e.g., from a Ministry Annual Report), because that source document was not available in this session. Only CMIE-tracked actual annual disbursement (Budget and Revised Estimates) is reported in §6. This gap should be filled before this paper is treated as final.
  • (f)The 2024-25 "Investments Completed - Cost" figure (₹76,619 crore, national total) is roughly ten times larger than any other year in the 2014-15 to 2025-26 series and was not independently re-verified against the underlying CMIE CapEx export in this session. It is reported as-is in Figure 10 but flagged here as a likely candidate for a data-entry or classification check before publication.
  • (g)The header text in row 4 and the "NATIONAL TOTALS" section header in the underlying ASI/State workbook's Transport Equipment tab both carry leftover text reading "Sector: Food & Agro-based Products" and "NIC Div 10, 11, 12", inconsistent with the tab's own title row, which correctly reads "SECTOR: Transport and Equipment | NIC: 29, 30". The underlying data values were checked against the Food & Agro tab and confirmed distinct (not a duplicated data pull), so this is treated as a stale copy-pasted label only; it should still be corrected at source.
